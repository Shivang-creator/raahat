Questions citizens and judges ask

Plain answers about safety, access, privacy and what this prototype can really do.

Is this triage safe?

Raahat does not diagnose. Code applies a small published rule file after you see and confirm the complaint. When a red flag is present, one flag is enough to route to Emergency.

What happens when there is an Emergency red flag?

The result says to go to Emergency now and not book an appointment. The rule ID is shown, and the nearest simulated emergency-capable hospital in your chosen locality is displayed.

Can someone who cannot read use it?

Yes. Complaint entry has three equal routes: speak, type, or tap the labelled 2D body map. Large buttons, keyboard focus and screen-reader labels are built into the flow.

What happens to my data?

This prototype keeps the complaint in the browser for this session and never stores it server-side. Typed words are sent to no service in the body-map route; free-text model reading is disclosed before use.

How is the wait time calculated?

It is simulated as slot start plus a simulated wait. Hospitals are sorted by estimated seen-time, then travel time, then name. It is not live queue data.

How is Raahat different from ORS?

ORS begins by asking you to choose a hospital and department. Raahat helps a citizen structure their own words first, then shows an honest simulated queue view. It does not connect to ORS.

Will this connect to ABDM?

No. ABDM, Aadhaar, ABHA, UHID, OTP and government systems are outside this prototype. Any future integration would need explicit consent, security review and a real public-service partnership.
